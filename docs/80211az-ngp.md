# 802.11az: Next-Generation Wi-Fi Positioning (NGP)

> Part of **Latent Radios**. Companion to [Fine Timing Measurement / RTT ranging](./ftm-rtt-ranging.md) (the 802.11mc predecessor), [UWB / FiRa ranging](./uwb-fira-ranging.md), and the [techniques reference](./techniques.md). Where 802.11mc made round-trip-time ranging deployable, **802.11az** makes it *secure, scalable, multi-user, and sub-meter* — and in doing so exposes an even richer set of PHY-layer measurements that matter for the sensing/repurposing story this catalog tracks.

---

## 1. What 802.11az is

**IEEE 802.11az** is the "Enhancements for positioning" amendment to the 802.11 standard, published as **IEEE Std 802.11az-2022** (task group chartered in 2015-2016; amendment approved 2022 and rolled into the base standard via 802.11-2024). It is marketed as **Next Generation Positioning (NGP)**.

It is the direct successor to **802.11mc Fine Timing Measurement (FTM)**. Where 11mc bolted a two-sided time-of-flight exchange onto the legacy management-frame path, 11az redesigns ranging around the modern OFDMA PHY (802.11ax / **HE**) and extends it to 802.11be / **EHT** (Wi-Fi 7). The four headline goals:

1. **Sub-meter accuracy** — a design target of **< 1 m** ranging error (and decimeter-class under favourable wideband/6 GHz conditions), versus the 1-2 m typically seen from 11mc FTM.
2. **Security** — a cryptographically protected physical-layer measurement (the **Secure LTF**) so an attacker cannot forge or shorten the measured distance. This is the enabling feature for keyless entry, digital car keys, payments and access control.
3. **Scalability / multi-user (MU)** — one AP can range **many** stations in a single trigger-based OFDMA round instead of a serial FTM burst per client.
4. **Efficiency and low power** — fewer frames per measurement, negotiated measurement schedules, and a passive-friendly design so battery clients can be located cheaply.

## 2. The two ranging protocols

802.11az defines two measurement procedures. Both estimate range from the **round-trip time (RTT)** between an *Initiating STA (ISTA)* and a *Responding STA (RSTA)*, using **Null Data Packet (NDP)** exchanges rather than the FTM action-frame/ACK loop of 11mc — which is where most of the overhead reduction comes from.

| | **NTB — Non-Trigger-Based** | **TB — Trigger-Based** |
|---|---|---|
| Who schedules | ISTA-driven (client initiates when it wants a fix) | RSTA/AP-driven via HE **Trigger** frames |
| Topology | Point-to-point, flexible, low latency | AP coordinates **many** clients at once |
| Multi-user | No (one pair per exchange) | **Yes** — UL OFDMA lets one round range multiple STAs |
| Best for | On-demand client positioning, unassociated ranging | Dense venues, asset tracking fleets, network-side location |
| Frame flow | NDP Announcement → I2R NDP → R2I NDP → (Location Measurement Report) | Trigger → UL NDP(s) → DL NDP → LMR |

Directional LTFs are labelled **I2R** (Initiator-to-Responder) and **R2I** (Responder-to-Initiator); each side timestamps the other's NDP and the RTT is reconstructed from the exchanged t1/t2/t3/t4 capture timestamps carried in the **Location Measurement Report (LMR)**.

## 3. The Secure LTF — the heart of 11az

The **Long Training Field (LTF)** is the known preamble symbol a receiver uses to estimate the channel and, crucially, the precise arrival time. In legacy FTM the LTF sequence is *fixed and public*, so an attacker who knows it can predict the waveform and mount **distance-reduction** ("early-detect / late-commit", a.k.a. cicada) or **distance-enlargement** attacks — fatal for anything that unlocks a door or a car.

802.11az replaces it with the **Secure HE-LTF (Secure LTF / SecLTF)**: the per-subcarrier LTF values are **randomized from a cryptographic sequence** derived from keys shared only by the two ranging peers. Because an attacker cannot predict the transmitted symbols, they cannot forge an earlier correlation peak. This is the Wi-Fi analogue of UWB's **Scrambled Timestamp Sequence (STS)** in [IEEE 802.15.4z](./uwb-fira-ranging.md).

Key establishment uses **PASN — Pre-Association Security Negotiation**: a lightweight authenticated key-exchange (an SAE/FILS-style handshake) that lets an **unassociated** station derive a pairwise key for secure ranging *without joining the BSS*. That matters because location clients frequently want to range an AP they are not associated to (or many APs for trilateration).

The security model protects **integrity of the distance estimate**; note that Secure LTF randomization *also* makes the ranging preamble far less useful to a passive third-party eavesdropper — a privacy win, and a genuine limitation for anyone hoping to passively harvest 11az ranging waveforms for sensing (see §8).

## 4. HE and EHT ranging, bandwidth, and where accuracy comes from

Ranging time resolution is bounded roughly by `c / (2·BW)`. Wider channels give sharper correlation peaks and better multipath separation:

| Channel BW | Single-shot range resolution (`c/2BW`) | Where available |
|---|---|---|
| 20 MHz | ~7.5 m | anywhere (legacy floor) |
| 80 MHz | ~1.9 m | 5 / 6 GHz |
| 160 MHz | ~0.94 m | 5 / 6 GHz (HE) |
| 320 MHz | ~0.47 m | **6 GHz, EHT / Wi-Fi 7** |

Raw resolution is only the starting point — 11az receivers combine **all subcarriers** (a wideband channel estimate) and apply super-resolution / first-path detection and phase to push well below the single-shot bound. The practical result: **160 MHz HE ranging** already reaches sub-meter under decent line-of-sight, and **320 MHz EHT ranging in 6 GHz** targets decimeter-class.

**Why 6 GHz is the enabler:** 1200 MHz of comparatively clean spectrum means 160/320 MHz channels are actually *usable*; **Standard Power** operation under **AFC** gives range; and the near-absence of legacy 802.11a/b/g/n traffic reduces the interference and multipath clutter that wideband ranging is sensitive to. 6 GHz + wide BW + Secure LTF is the combination that turns "Wi-Fi ranging" into a serious indoor-positioning primitive.

## 5. Multi-user (MU) ranging

The trigger-based (TB) mode reuses the 802.11ax **OFDMA uplink** machinery: an AP sends one Trigger frame and multiple stations reply with uplink ranging NDPs on assigned resource units, all inside a single TXOP. One AP can therefore obtain range to a **fleet of clients per measurement round**, instead of serially FTM-polling each one. Combined with negotiated measurement schedules (clients are told *when* their next ranging slot is and can sleep in between), this is what makes 11az viable for dense venues — malls, warehouses, airports, factory asset tracking — at low per-client airtime and power cost.

## 7. OS / API exposure

### Android — from `WifiRttManager` toward a unified `RangingManager`

- **Android 9 (API 28)** introduced `android.net.wifi.rtt` — `WifiRttManager`, `RangingRequest`, `RangingResult`, `ResponderConfig` — for **802.11mc FTM**. This is the foundation apps still use today.
- Subsequent releases layered **11az** onto the same package: capability probes for **non-trigger-based** ranging and **secure ranging** classes — notably `PasnConfig` and `SecureRangingConfig` (mapping directly to §3's PASN key establishment and Secure LTF). An app builds a `RangingRequest`, adds `ResponderConfig`s for each AP, and reads back distance + std-dev + RSSI, now with a secure-ranging path.
- **Android 16 (API 36, "Baklava")** adds the generic **`android.ranging` `RangingManager` / `RangingSession`** — a *technology-agnostic* ranging API that unifies **UWB**, **BLE Channel Sounding**, **Wi-Fi NAN RTT**, and **Wi-Fi RTT (11az)** under one session abstraction. This is the "RangingManager evolution": apps request "range to peer X at accuracy Y" and the framework picks/fuses the radios. (Verify exact API levels and class names against the live reference before coding — these moved across releases.)

Docs: [`WifiRttManager`](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/net/wifi/rtt/WifiRttManager), [`RangingRequest`](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/net/wifi/rtt/RangingRequest), [`ResponderConfig`](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/net/wifi/rtt/ResponderConfig), and the newer [`android.ranging`](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/ranging/package-summary) package.

### Apple — Nearby Interaction (UWB), and the Wi-Fi caveat

Apple's public precision-ranging API is **[Nearby Interaction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/nearbyinteraction)** (`NISession`, iOS 14+), which runs on the **U1** (iPhone 11+) / **U2** (iPhone 15+) **ultra-wideband** chips under IEEE 802.15.4z — the basis of AirTag "Precision Finding" and the Nearby Interaction Accessory Protocol. **This is UWB, not Wi-Fi 802.11az.** Apple participates in the ranging standards ecosystem and its Wi-Fi silicon supports FTM internally, but there is no public consumer 11az ranging API comparable to Android's `WifiRttManager`. For a Latent-Radios reader the takeaway is: on iOS, "sub-meter ranging" means UWB/Nearby Interaction; on Android it can mean Wi-Fi RTT/11az *or* UWB via the unified RangingManager.

## 8. Sensing implications (the Latent-Radios angle)

802.11az is a **ranging** amendment, but ranging and **sensing** share a physical substrate — the wideband channel estimate — so 11az deployments create a lot of exploitable measurement surface:

- **Rich, wideband channel estimates.** Each NDP exchange yields per-subcarrier complex channel data over up to 320 MHz — effectively high-resolution [CSI](./verification-tier2-csi.md) captured at ranging cadence. First-path time-of-flight plus phase is exactly the raw material used for through-wall presence, motion and gesture sensing.
- **Complement to 802.11bf.** The dedicated **WLAN Sensing** amendment (802.11bf) formalizes measurement exchanges for sensing; 11az's NDP sounding, trigger-based MU scheduling and dense reporting are close cousins and often ride the same hardware measurement engine. Expect vendors to expose 11az/bf measurements through the same driver hooks the [CSI toolchains](../projects/csi-toolchains.md) already target.
- **Secure LTF cuts both ways.** Randomizing the LTF *defends* the ranging integrity (§3) and simultaneously **denies a passive eavesdropper** a predictable preamble to correlate against — so 11az is, deliberately, *harder* to passively repurpose than open FTM. Third-party sensing off someone else's 11az secure ranging is largely foreclosed by design; sensing value accrues to the endpoints holding the keys.
- **No new SDR tier by itself.** Implementing 11az does not, on its own, move a chip up the [SDR ladder](./taxonomy.md) — it is a firmware/PHY feature, not open access to it. The value for repurposing is that 11az silicon (Wi-Fi 6E/7) carries the wideband capture and precise-timestamp hardware that a [nexmon](../projects/nexmon.md)-style patch could, in principle, expose as ranging/CSI measurements. Whether a given part's firmware surfaces raw LMR/CSI is the usual chip-by-chip question this catalog exists to answer.

| Overall status | shipping/mature | **early deployment on Wi-Fi 6E/7 (2024-2026)** |

## 10. Regulatory / safety note

802.11az ranging transmits ordinary 802.11 NDP/trigger frames within the device's existing Wi-Fi TX envelope — there is no special waveform or extra emission beyond normal Wi-Fi operation, so it inherits the host radio's regional certification (FCC/ETSI/etc.). The one deployment nuance is **6 GHz**: wideband **Standard Power** ranging must operate under **AFC** control, and outdoor/standard-power use is region-gated. There is nothing to "transmit at higher power" for better range — accuracy gains come from bandwidth and Secure LTF processing, not from exceeding regulatory limits. Do not attempt to spoof or replay another party's ranging exchanges; distance-manipulation attacks are exactly what Secure LTF exists to prevent, and defeating access-control ranging may be unlawful.
